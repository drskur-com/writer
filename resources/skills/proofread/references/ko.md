# Korean proofreading rules

Follow 한글 맞춤법, 표준어 규정, and 외래어 표기법 (National Institute of Korean Language).

## Commonly confused spellings
- 되/돼: 돼 is 되어. "안 돼요", "됐다", "되고".
- 안/않: 안 is 아니 (before a verb), 않 is 아니하 (after -지). "안 간다", "가지 않는다".
- -든지/-던지: -든지 is choice ("가든지 말든지"), -던지 is past recollection ("얼마나 춥던지").
- 로서/로써: 로서 is a role or status ("교사로서"), 로써 is a means ("대화로써").
- 웬/왠: 왠 only in 왠지 (왜인지). Otherwise 웬 ("웬일", "웬만하면").
- 맞히다/맞추다: 맞히다 is to hit or answer correctly ("정답을 맞히다"), 맞추다 is to fit or compare ("답을 맞춰 보다").
- 금세 (not 금새), 며칠 (not 몇일), 어떻게/어떡해 (어떡해 is 어떻게 해).

## Spacing (띄어쓰기)
- Bound nouns are written apart: 것, 수, 데, 만큼, 뿐, 지 (time since). "할 수 있다", "먹을 만큼", "온 지 사흘".
- When 만큼, 뿐, 대로 follow a noun they are particles and are attached: "너만큼", "너뿐".
- Particles attach to the preceding word: "학교에서부터", "너조차".
- Units are written apart from numbers written in Hangul ("한 개", "열 살"), but may attach to Arabic numerals ("3개", "10살").
- Auxiliary verbs are written apart in principle; attaching is also allowed ("먹어 보다" / "먹어보다"). Do not flag either; flag only inconsistency within the manuscript if it is frequent.

## Punctuation
- Use paired quotation marks consistently: either straight ("…") or curly (“…”) throughout. The app may convert them automatically, so do not flag the choice, only unpaired or mixed use.
- Ellipsis: "……" or "…" (also "..." is accepted). Flag only mixed use within the manuscript.
- No space before a period, comma, question mark, or exclamation mark.

## Leave alone
- Dialect, archaic words, onomatopoeia, and deliberate misspellings in dialogue.
- Spacing variants that the rules allow both ways.
